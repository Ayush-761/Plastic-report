Recycling Infrastructure

India's plastic recovery system is governed by the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016 and the EPR framework. Exact national recovery shares are not consistently reported, so this section focuses on the official regulatory system and state-level implementation data. See CPCB plastic waste page ↗ and MoEFCC on EPR ↗.

Plastic recovery context
Who handles plastic waste recovery in India?

Key Challenges - click to expand

Mixed waste streams contaminate recyclables. Without segregation, organic matter degrades plastic quality, so source separation remains a priority.
Thin films and multilayer plastics are difficult to recycle and need better source segregation and collection systems.
Implementation capacity varies widely by state and local body. CPCB and state boards continue to focus on registration, collection and processing capacity under the EPR framework.
Informal waste pickers play an important role in collection and sorting, but exact national recovery shares are not consistently reported in official statistics.
Monitoring and compliance remain uneven, which is why CPCB continues to publish portal registrations, inspection counts and fines under the EPR framework.

Environmental & Social Impacts

Source basis: PIB's explainer on adverse impacts of single-use plastics. The text below stays close to official language and avoids unsupported national percentages.

Hover or tap each card to reveal the detailed impact. SUPs affect every dimension of India's environment and public health.

Microplastics Alert Plastic pollution affects marine, soil and urban environments, but the exact percentages vary by study and are not presented here as a single official national statistic.
Official impact summary PIB's explainer on single-use plastics says plastic waste can contaminate soil and water, harm land, water and wildlife, clog open drains, and create air pollution when it is openly burned.
Source: PIB explainer on adverse impacts ↗
Social and economic impact The same official explainer notes welfare losses from littered public spaces and economic damage to tourism, shipping and fishing industries. It also says open burning can create health issues for vulnerable groups such as women, children and the elderly.

Government Policy & Regulations

Source basis: CPCB, PIB and the Gazette of India. The milestones below focus on rules, amendments and official portal data.

India's plastic regulatory framework is anchored in the Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016, the 2021 SUP ban, the 2022 EPR guidelines, the 2023 amendment for EPR implementation, and the 2025 labeling update. Click any event for full details. Read: PWM Rules 2022 ↗ · SUP ban announcement ↗

State-Level Initiatives

Maharashtra's buy-back mechanism printed refundable values on PET and PETE bottles under the state notification, linking producer responsibility with collection.
TNPCB reports about 96% urban plastic collection and 230 registered recycling units. The state banned 14 single-use plastic items in 2019.
CPCB's 2022-23 annual report identifies Karnataka as the largest plastic-waste generator among states, followed by Telangana and Tamil Nadu.
Kerala's local self-government system uses Haritha Karma Sena for door-to-door collection of non-bio waste, including plastic waste, across the state.
